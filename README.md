Key Modules & Methodology
          1. Present Value (PV) & Annuity Decision ModelingScenario: Consumer durable purchase evaluation (Upfront ₹32,000 vs. 8-year annuity of ₹6,000/year at 13% discount rate).   Formulas:Ordinary Annuity (End of Period): =PV(13%, 8, -6000, 0, 0) $\rightarrow$ ₹28,793.18   Annuity Due (Beginning of Period): =PV(13%, 8, -6000, 0, 1) $\rightarrow$ ₹32,536.29   Decision Rule: Opting for end-of-period installments yields the lowest present cost (saves ₹3,206.82 relative to upfront payment).  
          2. Loan Amortization & Repayment SchedulesHome Loan EMI Calculation (300 Months, 12% p.a.):Monthly rate: =12%/12 = 1.00%   Function: =PMT(1%, 300, -5000000, 0, 1) $\rightarrow$ ₹52,139.81 / month   8-Month Debt Schedule Breakdown:Evaluated dynamic shift between IPMT (decreasing interest component) and PPMT (increasing principal paydown).   Cumulative interest and principal between periods 2 and 3 cross-verified via =CUMIPMT(...) and =CUMPRINC(...).   Term and borrowing rate sensitivity modeled via =RATE() and =NPER().
          3. Investment Appraisal & Discounting ConventionsCash Flow Timing Sensitivities (20% Discount Rate):End-of-Year (Standard): =NPV(rate, cash_flows)   Beginning-of-Year (Immediate CF0): =CF0 + NPV(rate, CF1:CFn)   Mid-Year Discounting: =SQRT(1 + rate) * NPV(end_year)   Irregular Cash Flows: Modeled exact calendar dates via =XNPV(rate, values, dates) to capture precise day-count discounting. 
          4. Yield Dynamics: IRR, XIRR, and MIRRUnique vs. Multiple IRRs: Demonstrated how non-conventional cash flows (multiple sign reversals) yield multiple rates of return (-9.59% and 216.09%) or #NUM! errors.   NPV vs. IRR Conflict: Analyzed mutually exclusive projects with scale and timing disparities. Established why NPV takes precedence over IRR to maximize absolute wealth.   XIRR & MIRR: Applied =XIRR() for non-periodic venture cash flows and =MIRR() to separate distinct financing costs from reinvestment rates.
